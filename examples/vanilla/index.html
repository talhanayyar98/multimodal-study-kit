Reading task

Hydrothermal vents are openings in the sea floor where geothermally heated water emerges. They host ecosystems that do not depend on sunlight: chemosynthetic bacteria convert hydrogen sulfide into energy, and tube worms, clams and shrimp live off them.

The first vents were discovered in 1977 near the Galápagos Islands. Since then hundreds have been found along mid-ocean ridges, at depths of up to five kilometres.

Some researchers think that life on Earth may have begun at vents like these, because they offer energy, minerals and protection from the harsh conditions at the surface of the early planet.
